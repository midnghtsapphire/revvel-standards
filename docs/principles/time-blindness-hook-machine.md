# Time blindness meets the hook machine

> That's time blindness meeting a hook machine. ADHD brains lock onto fast, high-reward stuff, and those apps serve a new hit every 90 seconds, so your sense of time just stops running. It happens to almost everyone who watches them; they're designed for exactly that.

Audrey Evans, 2026-11-19, after losing track of time on a vertical micro-drama app. WR: [#18500](https://github.com/midnghtsapphire/revvel-standards/issues/18500).

Two uses. Same fact: the hook works, and the clock stops.

## Builders & agents

Expect time blindness. Do not trust the felt sense of time.

- **Time-box.** Start every task with a box (say 25 minutes) and a stop condition.
- **Show the clock.** Say the elapsed time at checkpoints. Do not let a session run unbounded.
- **Checkpoint.** At each box end: what is done, what is next, what is blocked. Stop or re-box on purpose.
- **End with a handoff.** Every session ends with the dated handoff (the daily memory handoff rule). Nothing lives only in the session.
- **Timer first.** Before opening a hook-heavy app, set an outside timer. Something outside the app has to pull you out.

## Products we make

Short episodes, cliffhangers and a free first taste are fair hooks for our own products (character IP, webtoons, micro-drama). Use them, with guardrails:

- **No dark patterns.** No fake countdowns, no hidden costs, no guilt buttons, no trick to stop someone leaving.
- **Clear pricing.** Show what the next episode costs before the tap.
- **Optional time reminders.** Offer "you've been here a while." Off by default is fine; easy to turn on is required.
- **A real stop point.** Each episode ends cleanly. The cliffhanger sells the next one; it does not trap the viewer.
- **No tracking, no affiliate code** unless Audrey asks for it.

Rule of thumb: build the hook, keep the exit.
