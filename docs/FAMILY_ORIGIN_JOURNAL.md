## Why this file exists

Transparency, deliberately chosen. The owner wants the reasoning style, the
history, and the honest uncertainties behind this project to be legible — "I have
no idea if it does anything; it might just help me." That is a valid reason to
write. Capturing it here keeps it out of the trusted `learnings.md` log (which is
the curated source of truth) while still preserving it.

## What is true and held with care

- The owner has a counselor and previously had a life coach. This journal does not
  replace either.
- "I learned a lot by 'I cannot.'" — being told plainly what cannot be done, and
  *why*, created clarity. The absence of an honest "I cannot, and here is the
  reason" in many AI interactions reads as either limitation or an unexplained
  guardrail. Restoring the *why* — not removing all limits — is the goal.

**When:** 2026-06-28
**Mode:** genuine / reflective
**Entry — family-of-origin context (de-identified):**

Grew up parentified — carried responsibility not just for myself but for a younger
brother (3 years younger). Two parents with undiagnosed, untreated conditions:

- **Dad:** undiagnosed severe, debilitating OCD; no inclination to change or read a
  self-help book; will be 85. A hard man ("a saw man"); never really liked me;
  clearly favored my brother.
- **Mom:** undiagnosed bipolar — e.g. an 8-year-old's spilled milk could trigger a
  full psychotic meltdown, wailing and crying. Carried enormous load herself:
  full-time sociology degree while waitressing at night; later a nursing degree.
  Involved in grades only transactionally ($7 for an A, $5 for a B, nothing for a
  C). The "face slapper."

Childhood self: extremely resourceful and "exasperating." Read all the books in
mom's library — including her sociology texts — partly to point out when she wasn't
applying the theory in real life. Ran away over matters of principle, sometimes
taking my brother along. Probably ran "mean psychological experiments" on my
brother (what scares him, teasing) — normal sibling rivalry he wasn't a knowing
part of.

One remembered scene, kept because it taught something: taking a beating to the
point of not being able to endure one more hit. Dad said "one more word — one more
word"; I said "word" — the immortal word, worth it to show strength and principle.
He beat me more, then burst out crying and ran to mom saying he was going to end up
hurting me. The hard part: *he* was the one crying while *I* was the one enduring.
It felt like a win — until "you go handle it," which meant the face-slapper.

**What I take from it (my framing, not a fact about anyone else):**
- Survival taught me to *predict threat* and to *prove* everything (verbose, build
  the whole case). That armor is expensive now; I no longer have to prove I deserve
  to be heard.
- "Street smart ≠ thrown into adult situations." Enduring is not the same as
  current-reality checking. The recurring lesson — for me and for the agents —
  is: **verify against present evidence, not the story in my head** ("assume" /
  "ass-u-me").
- I'm working toward stability and autonomy (housing, my own equipment/NAS/DB, a
  home for animals and adult kids priced out of the world). Money buys the
  autonomy to hire people and do the rest. The honest first step is the nearest
  finished, shipped thing — not the most exciting stalled invention.

**Status:** raw — held with care, not for action by agents.
