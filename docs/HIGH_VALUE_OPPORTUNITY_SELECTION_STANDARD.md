# High-Value Opportunity Selection Standard

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Active  
**Owner:** @midnghtsapphire  
**Last Updated:** 2026-06-14

---

## Purpose

This repository is optimized for high-value product creation, not low-value
artifact churn.

The research engine must preserve the business and motivation system that ties
ideas to strategic goals, immediate revenue potential, candidate comparison,
weighted selection, and probable income.

---

## Core Strategic Rule

Default policy:

- prioritize products that support the long-range **$10,000,000 objective**, and
- prefer products with plausible immediate revenue potential of at least
  **$3,000**,
- avoid creating or prioritizing low-value products unless they are required
  infrastructure for a higher-value product or provide documented strategic
  leverage.

This rule applies to research, recommendation, implementation prioritization,
and fix-first process decisions.

---

## Opportunity Selection Engine Rule

When an idea, WR, or request can become more than one meaningful product or
artifact, the research process must evaluate **at least 3 candidate options**.

These candidates may be completely different artifact types.

Examples:

- SaaS / website,
- CLI,
- API,
- MCP server,
- PDF,
- Chrome extension,
- internal automation,
- skill,
- service or productized deliverable.

---

## Candidate Comparison Requirement

Each candidate should include, when applicable:

- candidate name,
- artifact type,
- problem solved,
- target buyer or user,
- strategic alignment,
- immediate revenue potential,
- low / base / high probable monthly income,
- time-to-revenue,
- effort or complexity,
- confidence or evidence level,
- weighted qualification score.

---

## 80% Qualification Threshold Rule

The default recommendation should favor the candidate that best clears the
high-value qualification threshold.

Default threshold:

- **80% weighted qualification score**, or
- documented override with explanation.

The threshold exists to prevent drift into low-value work that looks busy but
does not materially support strategic goals.

---

## Immediate Revenue Qualification Rule

A product should generally move forward only if it qualifies as one of the
following:

1. **Immediate Revenue Qualified**
   - plausible path to at least **$3,000** near-term value.
2. **Strategic Platform Qualified**
   - not immediate revenue by itself, but clearly supports the longer-range
     **$10,000,000** objective.
3. **Infrastructure Exception**
   - required dependency for a higher-value product.

If a candidate fits none of these, it should be deprioritized, deferred,
archived, or marked as research-only.

---

## Motivation Preservation Rule

Probable income and contribution estimates are required when applicable.

These estimates are used not only for prioritization, but also to preserve
operator momentum and reduce abandonment of viable product paths.

The system must preserve the motivational value of visible probable income
without confusing speculative estimates for sourced facts.

---

## Revenue Contribution Requirement

When a WR or idea is product-, revenue-, or distribution-oriented, it must
include a contribution estimate section containing:

- strategic goal alignment,
- immediate revenue qualification,
- low / base / high contribution estimate,
- time horizon,
- key assumptions,
- evidence or confidence level,
- kill, defer, or downgrade criteria.

Clearly separate:

- sourced facts,
- directional assumptions,
- speculative upside.

---

## Artifact Flexibility Rule

The same idea may validly map to different artifact shapes. The research engine
must allow those shapes to differ and compare them on merit rather than forcing
a single predetermined format too early.

A strong research packet should be able to say:

- the idea could become a SaaS,
- or a CLI,
- or an MCP,
- or a PDF,
- and here is which one currently scores highest.

---

## Non-Removal Rule

The opportunity scoring, probable income, contribution estimate, and
candidate-comparison system are business-critical logic.

They must not be silently removed from standards, templates, or process docs.
If revised or superseded, preserve history and document the replacement.
