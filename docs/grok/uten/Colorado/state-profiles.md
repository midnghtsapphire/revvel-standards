# State-by-State Data Center Profiles & Heat Reuse Potential

**Focus**: Electricity consumption, water use, and potential for BNAT-UTEN-style heat recovery to serve building heating needs.

Data compiled from LBNL, EPRI, industry reports, and 2026 analyses. Estimates for recoverable heat assume 45–55% of total electricity can be recovered as useful thermal energy with modern liquid cooling + HTHPs.

## Detailed Profiles

### Virginia (Highest Priority for National Replication)
- **Hub**: Ashburn / Loudoun County ("Data Center Alley")
- **Current Impact**: ~24 TWh electricity (~25%+ of state power in some analyses). Significant local water use.
- **Heat Reuse Potential**: 10.5–12 TWh thermal recoverable. Could heat 85,000–110,000 homes equivalent or large commercial districts.
- **BNAT-UTEN Fit**: Existing infrastructure + high concentration makes network buildout attractive. Strong candidate for early large-scale deployment.

### Texas
- **Hubs**: Dallas-Fort Worth (dominant), Austin, San Antonio.
- **Current Impact**: 17 TWh. Major growth projected; significant ERCOT grid impact.
- **Heat Reuse Potential**: 7.5–9 TWh thermal. Could serve tens of thousands of homes + commercial.
- **BNAT-UTEN Fit**: Large scale opportunities; water stress in parts of state makes efficiency critical.

### Arizona
- **Hubs**: Phoenix metro (Goodyear, Mesa, Chandler)
- **Current Impact**: 10.5 TWh. High water stress area.
- **Heat Reuse Potential**: 4.2–5.5 TWh thermal. Direct community benefit in desert climate.
- **BNAT-UTEN Fit**: Highest urgency due to combined power + water constraints.

### Colorado (Primary Focus of This Initiative)
- **Hubs**: Denver metro / Aurora (QTS campus etc.), Colorado Springs, emerging large sites near Windsor/Fort Collins.
- **Current Impact**: ~57 facilities, est. 3.5 TWh electricity, ~600M gallons water/year.
- **Heat Reuse Potential with BNAT-UTEN**: 1.4–1.9 TWh thermal → 12,000–16,000+ homes equivalent. Significant water savings (150–250M gallons/year estimated).
- **Advantages**: Cold climate = excellent heating demand match. Water-stressed Front Range. Growing data center presence without overwhelming existing infrastructure yet.
- **Recommended First Steps**: Pilot in Aurora/Denver area with connection to nearby commercial or residential developments.

### Other Notable States
- **Georgia**: Fast growth; good candidate for Southeast replication.
- **California**: Policy leadership potential; strong incentives possible.
- **Illinois & Oregon**: Notable consumption with specific local water/power issues.

## Methodology Notes
- Electricity figures primarily from 2026 ElectricChoice / industry mapping.
- Water estimates derived from national ~17B gallon total allocated proportionally + known high-impact locations.
- Recoverable heat: Conservative 45–55% of electricity as useful thermal output with modern liquid cooling + HTHPs.
- Building equivalent: Based on average US residential heating load (~10–15 MWh thermal per home/year).

This data supports prioritization: **Virginia and Texas** for scale; **Arizona and Colorado** for urgency and fit with BNAT-UTEN water-positive design.
