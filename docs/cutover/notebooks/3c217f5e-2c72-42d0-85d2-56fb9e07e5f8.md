## Faithful content summary of the visible source text and opened existing report
The sources describe Pure Deterministic Cognition (PDC) as a standalone, rule-bound method that maps meaning-bearing input through declared semantic axes, deterministic scoring, relation mapping, indexing, denotum rendering, and mandatory audit/revision. The minimal pipeline is represented as term/input → axes → score → relations → index → denotum → audit. The text says fixed input plus fixed rules should produce fixed output; PDC is not based on neural weights, machine learning, LLMs, stochastic generation, hidden intuition, or consciousness. An LLM may optionally suggest terms, compare meanings, help render language, or adversarially review, but is not the engine or final authority.

A denotum is a compressed, explicit, auditable meaning-unit; plural: denota. The indexed denotum registry orders them by declared semantic weight. The source says lower index means greater declared weight and index zero must be declared as an origin attractor. A denotum should preserve core meaning, distinction, relation, force, risk, boundary, and use; relation metadata can include near/parent/child/contrast/risk/translation terms and forbidden conflations. Compression must not mutilate meaning.

Semantic weight is calculated across declared axes. Examples in the source include existential force, affective charge, governance force, relational density, consequence, cultural specificity, translation risk, embodiment, temporal depth, institutional weight, ordinary function, and frequency. It gives illustrative axis values/weights, not validated empirical metrics: existential_force 1.00, affective_charge 0.95, consequence 0.90, relational_density 0.85, governance_force 0.80, temporal_depth 0.75, cultural_specificity 0.70, translation_risk 0.65, institutional_weight 0.60, embodiment 0.55, ordinary_function 0.25, frequency 0.20. It describes a weighted sum plus relation/consequence/governance bonuses and ambiguity/dilution penalties; the formula and weights must be declared before use and may vary by domain.

Governance/audit provisions emphasize declared axes, weights, scoring, relation/index rules, authority, evidence, and documented revision history. The mandatory audit checks include frequency collapse, cultural flattening, false objectivity, over-personalisation, poetic drift, technical vagueness, and forbidden conflation. Revisions require a reason and preserve history. The text warns that deterministic does not mean true, indexed does not mean universal, and compressed does not mean complete. Cultural examples distinguish Tiriti from a generic “treaty” and protect Māori concepts such as mana, rangatiratanga, kaitiaki, tikanga, and whakapapa from flattening. The glossary includes 250 numbered concepts spanning existence/logic, system operations, cognition, social structures, governance, Māori concepts, and interdependence.
