# Models and parental responsibility

The publisher of Robert J. Koester, Lost Person Behavior, states that the book presents 41 subject categories. The distance tables are copyrighted. This course does not copy them. Forty-one is not an element of a Colorado offense.

Autism and dementia are different categories in that taxonomy. Koester’s 2023 Journal of Search and Rescue paper, using ISRID2, reports that people with autism spectrum disorder were 3–4 times more likely to require a search than the comparison group, and that autistic children were not statistically different from autistic adults on the spatial comparison he ran. A hiker ring used for either category is a false premise in a declaration.

Section 24-33.5-415.8 is the current Colorado alert for a missing senior, a missing person with a developmental disability, and a missing person with a dementia disease or related disability. The local agency shall require documentation. After it verifies a developmental-disability disappearance, it may notify CBI. When notified and after it confirms the information, CBI shall issue the alert. “May” is not “shall.” Rule 8 CCR 1507-26 is where autism appears in the verification definition. The federal developmental-disability definition does not apply to that rule. Read both before you tell a client the alert is automatic.

CBI’s public wanderer page describes Senate Bill 22-187 grant funding for local recovery programs covering Alzheimer’s and related dementias, autism, brain injury, and developmental, cognitive, neurological, or chromosomal disorders. In 2025 CBI reports it issued 64 senior alerts and 10 developmental-disability alerts. An alert is not a substitute for the report under § 16-2.7-102.

An autistic missing-person category is not a mass-shooter profile. A dementia category is not one either. Using either as a prediction of violence is the bias error this course already forbids.
