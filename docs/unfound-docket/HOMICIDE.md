## Colorado charge is the mental state

| Offense | Statute | What must be proved |
|---|---|---|
| First-degree murder | § 18-3-102(1)(a) or (1)(f) | After deliberation and with intent, or a knowing killing of a child under 12 by a person in a position of trust. Class 1 felony. |
| Second-degree murder | § 18-3-103(1)(a) | Knowingly causes the death. Class 2. Sudden heat of passion, if proved, is a class 3 under § 18-3-103(3)(b). It is not manslaughter. |
| Manslaughter | § 18-3-104(1)(a) | Recklessly causes the death. Class 4. Colorado has no separate voluntary-manslaughter label. |
| Criminally negligent homicide | § 18-3-105 | Causes death by criminal negligence. Class 5. Confirm the current section before you cite the penalty. |
| Child abuse resulting in death | § 18-6-401(7)(a) | Knowing or reckless abuse resulting in death is a class 2, unless § 18-3-102(1)(f) makes it first-degree murder. Criminal negligence resulting in death is a class 3. The prosecution must prove the death resulted from the abuse. Read People v. Ceus, No. 24SC508 (Colo. June 29, 2026), before you treat death as a mere label. |

A La Plata County jury convicted Mark Redwine, the father, of second-degree murder and child abuse resulting in death. The child had been missing. That conviction is murder, not manslaughter. People v. Redwine, No. 22CA0596 (Colo. App. Apr. 9, 2026), is an unpublished restitution order under C.A.R. 35(e). It is not precedent. It recites the verdict.

Death can be proved without a recovered body. People v. Bennett, 183 Colo. 125, 515 P.2d 466 (1973). Behavioral victimology is not that proof. Salcedo v. People, 999 P.2d 833 (Colo. 2000).
