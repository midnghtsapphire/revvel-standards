# Spectrum Blueprint — Read-Through

**Source:** uncited infographic (Drive). Tags below are an assessment of
claim-standing, **not** a literature verification. No citations fabricated.

Speculative material is **kept**, not deleted, for reading and research.

---

## Load-bearing (what the bench actually uses)

- **[PROVEN]** 660 nm and 850 nm are the two most-studied photobiomodulation
  bands in peer-reviewed human trials.
- **[PROVEN]** Dose is fluence (J/cm²) × time, not power alone; over-dosing
  reverses benefit (biphasic response, Hamblin et al.).
- **[PROVEN]** Ocular exposure to high-irradiance NIR requires eye
  protection; retinal thermal limits are well-characterized (ICNIRP).
- **[EMERGING]** 1064 nm penetrates deeper than 850 nm but has less
  clinical corpus; promising for musculoskeletal, thin evidence for CNS.
- **[EMERGING]** Pulsed vs. CW delivery may matter for mitochondrial
  response; mechanism debated, clinical delta small.

## Aspirational (kept for research, not wired into the dose engine)

- **[SPECULATIVE]** Specific frequency "resonance" claims (e.g., 40 Hz
  gamma entrainment via light) — one strong mouse study, human replication
  mixed. Do not encode as a dose recommendation.
- **[SPECULATIVE]** UV-A microdosing for circadian anchoring — mechanism
  plausible, dose window narrow, skin-cancer risk non-trivial.
- **[SPECULATIVE]** Full-spectrum "sunlight-equivalent" indoor panels
  matching solar SPD — marketing claim; no clinical endpoint studies.
- **[SPECULATIVE]** Infrared sauna "detox" claims — thermal benefit real,
  detox mechanism unsupported.

## BLANK

The infographic leaves the following unaddressed. The bench does **not**
fill these in with guesses:

- Interaction with photosensitizing medications.
- Pediatric dosing.
- Pregnancy safety windows.
- Long-term (>5 yr) cumulative exposure data for consumer devices.
- Genetic variance in response (e.g., mitochondrial haplogroups).

## Reading protocol

When a claim moves from `[SPECULATIVE]` → `[EMERGING]`, it earns a note in
`wr/research/`. When it moves to `[PROVEN]`, it earns a slot in the dose
engine with a citation. Never the reverse order.
