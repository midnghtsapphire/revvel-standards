# Spectrum Blueprint — Read-Through

**Source:** Uncited infographic (Drive). Tags below assess **claim-standing**, not literature verification. **No citations are fabricated.**

## Legend

- `[PROVEN]` — Consensus in peer-reviewed literature; mechanism established.
- `[EMERGING]` — Active research area with converging but non-consensus evidence.
- `[SPECULATIVE]` — Hypothesis, extrapolation, or marketing claim without established mechanism.

Speculative material is **kept, not deleted** — this document is for reading and research, not shipping.

## Load-bearing (informs current builds)

- Circadian entrainment by ~480 nm light via ipRGC/melanopsin. `[PROVEN]`
- Melatonin suppression thresholds vary with intensity, duration, and prior light history. `[PROVEN]`
- Flicker (PWM) below ~200 Hz is detectable by a nontrivial fraction of the population and correlates with headache/eyestrain reports. `[EMERGING]`
- Blue-light hazard (BLH) weighting peaks near 435–440 nm; standard IEC 62471 risk-group classification applies. `[PROVEN]`
- Melanopic EDI (CIE S 026) is the current best single metric for non-visual effects. `[PROVEN]`

## Aspirational (interesting, not yet load-bearing)

- Specific narrow-band wavelengths for mitochondrial "photobiomodulation" of cognition. `[EMERGING]`
- Red/NIR (~660/850 nm) effects on skin, wound healing, and hair. `[EMERGING]`
- Violet (~400–410 nm) for myopia progression. `[EMERGING]`
- Green (~530 nm) analgesia for migraine. `[EMERGING]`
- Discrete "resonance frequencies" for organs. `[SPECULATIVE]`
- Claims that room-scale ambient light replicates clinical PBM dosages. `[SPECULATIVE]`
- Any claim of curing systemic disease via consumer lighting. `[SPECULATIVE]`

## Reading notes

- The infographic conflates **dose** (irradiance × time × spectrum) with **wavelength alone**. Any downstream product must specify all three.
- Where the source uses the word "heals," downgrade to "modulates" pending mechanism.
- Where the source uses "resonance," downgrade to `[SPECULATIVE]` absent a cited mechanism.
