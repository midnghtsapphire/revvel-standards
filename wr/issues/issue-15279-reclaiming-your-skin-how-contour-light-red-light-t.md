## Executive Summary

Post-pregnancy stretch marks affect an estimated 50–90% of pregnant women ([NCBI, 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5325738/)), making this one of the largest underserved cosmetic concerns in the postpartum wellness space. Red light therapy (RLT) — particularly devices like the Contour Light system — is clinically supported for collagen stimulation and skin rejuvenation, positioning it as a compelling non-invasive option for stretch mark reduction.

The global red light therapy market was valued at USD 533.8 million in 2025 and is projected to reach USD 1.13 billion by 2033 at a CAGR of 9.8% ([Research and Markets, 2025](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/6241239/red-light-therapy-market-size-share-and-trends)). The stretch marks treatment market sits at USD 2.51 billion in 2024, expected to reach USD 3.57 billion by 2031 at a CAGR of approximately 4.5% ([Verified Market Research, 2024](https://www.verifiedmarketresearch.com/product/stretch-marks-treatment-market/)).

The proposed product is a **postpartum red light therapy companion app** — a freemium web and mobile tool that provides:

- Protocol guides based on Contour Light / RLT session parameters
- Session tracking and progress logging
- Before/after photo journaling
- Affiliate-linked device and skincare product recommendations
- Educational content on postpartum skin recovery

This is a well-differentiated niche in the crowded wellness app space, with strong affiliate monetization potential (Joovv, Mito Red, CurrentBody) and clear SEO keyword targets in the postpartum health vertical.

## Appendix A — Regulatory & Compliance Framework

> **Note:** This appendix addresses two independent regulatory tracks. Prior guidance conflated medical disclaimers with privacy law compliance — these are separate obligations that must be assessed independently.

### Track 1 — Privacy & Data Classification (HIPAA / GDPR / CCPA)

**Step 1: Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) — required before cloud sync or any feature expanding data collection**

Before enabling any cloud-sync or server-side health-data storage, engage privacy counsel to document:

- What data is collected (postpartum weeks, stretch mark severity ratings, symptom notes, photos, device usage).
- Whether the entity operating the app qualifies as a **Covered Entity** or **Business Associate** under 45 CFR §160.103. HIPAA applies to Covered Entities and Business Associates; most direct-to-consumer health apps fall outside HIPAA — but this applies only when the entity is neither a CE/BA *nor* handling data on behalf of one. An app that sources or processes health data from a healthcare provider may still trigger obligations regardless of its own classification.
- Whether any collected data constitutes **Protected Health Information (PHI)** in the hands of a Covered Entity or Business Associate.
- Whether GDPR Article 9 (special category health data) and/or CCPA/CPRA sensitive personal information rules apply based on the actual territorial and data-processing nexus for this product.

**Step 2: Decision Point**

Based on the PIA, one of two paths must be formally documented:

#### Path A — Entity qualifies as Covered Entity or Business Associate (HIPAA applies)

If the PIA concludes the operating entity is a Covered Entity or Business Associate and handles PHI, a HIPAA compliance program is required before launch. The table below separates statutory obligations from recommended project controls:

| Category | HIPAA Statutory Requirement | Recommended Project Control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business Associate Agreements | BAAs required with vendors that **create, receive, maintain, or transmit PHI on behalf of** the Covered Entity or Business Associate (45 CFR §164.308(b)) — not every third-party processor | Identify which vendors touch PHI; execute BAAs with those vendors; document isolation strategy for vendors deliberately kept outside PHI scope |
| Administrative Safeguards | Risk analysis; risk management program; sanctions policy; workforce training (§164.308) | Designated Privacy and Security Officers; documented risk register |
| Physical Safeguards | Device and media controls; facility access policies (§164.310) | Applied to any on-prem infrastructure |
| Technical Safeguards | Access controls; audit controls; integrity controls; transmission security (§164.312) | Encryption at rest and in transit (project selects AES-256/TLS 1.2+ as implementation); unique user IDs; automatic logoff |
| Audit Logging | Mechanisms to record and examine system activity (§164.312(b)) | Tamper-evident logs; §164.316 requires policies and procedures retained for 6 years (this applies to compliance documentation, not log data — actual log retention periods are governed by applicable state record-retention laws and organizational risk analysis) |
| Breach Notification | Individual notice ≤ 60 days; HHS notice; media notice for breaches ≥ 500 individuals (§164.400–414) | Documented incident response runbook; tested annually |
| Workforce Training | Training required; records retained (§164.308(a)(5)) | Completed before PHI access is granted; refreshed annually |
| Patient Rights | Rights of access, amendment, accounting, restriction (§164.524–528) | Mechanisms built into user account management |
| Policies & Procedures | Written, versioned (§164.316) | Version-controlled policy documents |

#### Path B — Entity does not qualify as Covered Entity or Business Associate

If the PIA concludes HIPAA does not apply, **the reasoning must be explicitly documented**, including:

- Why the operating entity is not a Covered Entity or Business Associate.
- Which data elements were reviewed and how each is classified.
- Sign-off from qualified privacy counsel.
- A commitment to re-run the PIA if entity status or data collection scope changes.

Under Path B, assess GDPR and CCPA/CPRA applicability independently based on actual territorial nexus, data flows, and applicable business thresholds and exemptions — these do not apply automatically.

**Step 3: Data Minimization (both paths)**

- Collect the minimum data necessary for the stated feature.
- Default to on-device storage; require explicit, granular consent for any cloud sync.
- Provide export and deletion tools to support applicable user rights (GDPR Article 17, CCPA §1798.105 where applicable).
- No sale of user data.

### Track 2 — FDA SaMD Risk Mitigation (independent from Track 1)

This track addresses whether the app functions as **Software as a Medical Device (SaMD)** under FDA guidance. It is separate from — and does not affect — the privacy obligations assessed in Track 1.

FDA device status depends on intended use, claims, labeling, and functionality. It cannot be established by declaration alone. The following design choices support a general-wellness positioning that reduces SaMD classification risk, subject to a formal assessment if functionality expands:

- No diagnostic claims. No treatment recommendations. No dosage guidance.
- No algorithmic severity scoring presented as a clinical assessment.
- Educational content clearly framed as general wellness information.
- Progress tracking is user-driven journaling, not clinical measurement.

**Wellness disclaimers (required, but NOT a substitute for Track 1 compliance):**

- "This app is for general wellness and educational purposes only."
- "This app does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition."
- "Consult a licensed healthcare provider before starting any therapy, especially postpartum."
- Disclaimers appear at onboarding, in-app, and in Terms of Service.

### Track 3 — Other Applicable Regimes (applicability to be assessed)

The following regimes may apply depending on entity status, data flows, and product jurisdiction. Each requires an independent applicability determination:

- **Washington My Health My Data Act (MHMDA)** — assess whether the product's consumer health data collection and territorial nexus fall within the Act's scope and exemptions.
- **California Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (CMIA)** — assess whether the app meets the provider/software and medical-information definitions that trigger CMIA obligations.
- **FTC Health Breach Notification Rule** — assess whether the app qualifies as a vendor of personal health records or a related entity under the Rule's specific definitions.
- **App Store / Play Store health data policies** — separate contractual obligations from Apple and Google that apply regardless of regulatory status.
- **State biometric laws** (BIPA, etc.) — relevant if Phase 2 photo analysis uses ML-based biometric identification.
